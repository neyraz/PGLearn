Foundation

Muropeptide Structure & Chemistry

PG is a single, covalently closed bag-shaped macromolecule encasing the cell. Understanding its architecture is the prerequisite for understanding all ~40 enzymes that build, remodel, and repair it.

Muropeptide monomer, cross-link chemistry, and glycan strand. Dashed ⑤ = removed in mature PG.
🧱
The Sacculus
A single, enormous bag-shaped macromolecule. E. coli: ~2–7 nm thick, 1–3 layers. B. subtilis: ~20–80 nm, multilayered. Must resist ~3–5 atm (Gram−) or ~15–20 atm (Gram+) turgor pressure.
⛓️
Stem Peptide
L-Ala(1)→γ-D-Glu(2)→mDAP/L-Lys(3)→D-Ala(4)→D-Ala(5). D-amino acids resist host proteases. The γ-D-Glu iso-peptide bond is also protease-resistant. Position 3 determines cross-link architecture.
🔗
Two Cross-link Types
DD (4→3): ~85–95% of crosslinks in growing E. coli; β-lactam-sensitive. Also written "3→4" depending on whether donor or acceptor is named first. LD (3→3): ~5–15% in growing E. coli, rising sharply in stationary phase and under envelope stress. Dominant crosslink type in mycobacteria and Clostridiales. Carbapenem-sensitive only.
🔬
Bacteria-Unique Chemistry
MurNAc, mDAP, and D-amino acids are absent from eukaryotes. Every PG enzyme is therefore a theoretically safe and selective antibiotic target — explaining why ~half of all clinical antibiotics target PG.
📏
Glycan Chain Length
Average ~30 disaccharides in E. coli. MltG acts as an endolytic terminase. Strand length affects PG mechanics and hydrolase susceptibility.
🛡️
PG Modifications
O-acetylation of MurNAc: blocks lysozyme. N-deacetylation of GlcNAc: also blocks lysozyme. mDAP amidation: reduces negative charge. Key virulence factors and new antibiotic targets.

2024 Discovery: UptA — The UndP Inner-Leaflet Flipper
A second lipid carrier flipper, UptA, was identified in B. subtilis. After transglycosylation releases UndPP, phosphatases dephosphorylate it to UndP, and UptA flips UndP back to the cytoplasmic leaflet where MraY requires it — closing the carrier recycling cycle. Represents a new drug target distinct from MurJ.
Macromolecular Machines

Elongasome & Divisome

PG enzymes are organised into two dynamic multiprotein complexes. These are the spatial logic of the cell wall — controlling where and when each enzyme acts.

🔄 Elongasome (Rod Complex)

Drives lateral sidewall PG synthesis along the cell cylinder. RodA–PBP2 processive movement is guided by MreB actin-like cytoskeletal rotation.
Components (cytoplasm → OM):
MreB (actin-like)MreCMreDRodZ RodA (TGase)PBP2 (TPase) PBP1A (gap filler)LpoA (OM activator)
Core Mechanism
RodA–PBP2 synthesis activity drives MreB rotation. MreC activates both RodA TGase and PBP2 TPase. PBP1A fills gaps, activated by LpoA spanning from the outer membrane.

✂️ Divisome

Drives septal PG synthesis at mid-cell during division. FtsZ treadmilling distributes synthases; FtsN triggers activation; FtsW/PBP3 synthesis drives constriction.
Assembly order (cytoplasm → OM):
FtsZ (tubulin-like)FtsAZipAFtsEXFtsK FtsQLBFtsW (TGase)PBP3 (TPase) PBP1BFtsN (trigger)LpoB (activator)
Two-Track Model (Yang et al. 2021, Nat. Microbiol.)
Single-molecule imaging: two FtsW populations — Z-track (~30 nm/s, inactive, follows FtsZ) and sPG-track (~8 nm/s, active synthesis). FtsN arrival triggers switch. B. subtilis has only a single sPG-track (2024).
🔑 SEDS–bPBP Pairing Principle
RodA pairs exclusively with PBP2 (elongasome); FtsW pairs exclusively with PBP3 (divisome). SEDS TGase activity requires its cognate bPBP for activation. This paired activation ensures polymerisation only occurs where cross-linking also occurs.
Part II

PG Remodelling

Hydrolases continuously open the existing PG mesh, allowing insertion of new material, daughter cell separation, and PG repair. Without them, growth is impossible. They are now emerging antibiotic targets.

2025 Research: Hydrolases as Antibiotic Targets (ACS Infect. Dis. 2025)
Inhibiting the NlpC/P60 endopeptidase SagA in VREfm using first-in-class covalent sulfonyl fluoride inhibitors sensitises it to vancomycin — even though the vanA operon is still intact. SagA inhibition improved outcomes in mouse models of VREfm sepsis. This establishes PG hydrolases as a new antibiotic target class: block remodelling to restore antibiotic susceptibility.
Part III

Recycling & Repair

~60% of sidewall PG is recycled every generation in E. coli. This closed loop is metabolically efficient and directly connected to β-lactamase induction — making it a therapeutic target.

PG recycling — closed loop from sacculus degradation back to synthesis. Also feeds β-lactamase induction.
Recycling Enzymes — click for detail
AmpG + MppA-Opp + YejBEF-YepA (importers) AmpD (anhMurNAc–L-Ala amidase) NagZ LdcA / LdcV / ElsL Mpl (murein peptide ligase) Peptide catabolism (MpaA·YcjG·PepD) Sugar salvage (AnmK·MurQ·NagK·NagA)
⚠️ Recycling Architecture Determines Antibiotic Susceptibility
Some Pseudomonadota bypass the GlcNAc route entirely: MupP dephosphorylates MurNAc-6-P, AmgK 1-phosphorylates it, and MurU activates it with UTP to give UDP-MurNAc directly. Because this re-enters the pathway downstream of MurA and MurB, it confers intrinsic fosfomycin resistance — the drug's target is simply skipped. Separately, blocking NagZ or AmpG suppresses AmpC β-lactamase induction without inhibiting the β-lactamase itself: an anti-resistance strategy rather than a direct antibacterial one.
🔧 LD-TPase Repair Pathway
Step 1 — New Glycan
PBP1B (TGase) inserts a new glycan chain at the defect site. Activated by LpoB from the OM.
Step 2 — Trimming
DD-carboxypeptidases (especially PBP6B at acidic pH) remove D-Ala⑤ → tetrapeptide substrate for LDTs.
Step 3 — LD Cross-linking
LdtD/E/F form 3→3 LD cross-links (β-lactam resistant). LdtD is Cpx-regulon controlled.
Comparative Microbiology

Gram-Negative vs Gram-Positive

The core Mur pathway is conserved, but major structural and regulatory differences define each group's PG architecture and antibiotic susceptibility.

Gram-Negative (E. coli)

Thin PG: 2–7 nm, 1–3 layers, located in periplasm between IM and OM
Position 3 AA: mDAP — enables direct 4→3 and 3→3 cross-links without bridging peptides
Outer membrane: Lpp covalently anchored to PG by LdtA/B/C; OmpA also connects
Lpo activation: LpoA activates PBP1A; LpoB activates PBP1B (trans-periplasm)
Endopeptidases: MepS/M/H redundantly essential; MepS regulated by NlpI–Prc
GlmS regulation: sRNA cascade (GlmY→GlmZ→Hfq→glmS mRNA) via QseE/QseF/RapZ
AmpG recycling: feeds β-lactamase induction via AmpR→ampC
Divisome: Two-track model (Z-track + sPG-track); FtsN triggers switch

Gram-Positive (B. subtilis / S. aureus)

Thick PG: 20–80 nm, multilayered, exterior to single cytoplasmic membrane
Position 3 AA: mDAP (B. subtilis) or L-Lys (S. aureus, S. pneumoniae)
Bridging peptides: L-Lys requires interbridge — pentaglycine (S. aureus, by FemA/B/X)
No outer membrane: PG decorated with WTA (wall teichoic acid) and LTA
FtsEX activation: CwlO + LytE (B. subtilis); PcsB (S. pneumoniae) — no Lpo proteins
GlmS ribozyme: Self-cleaving glmS mRNA ribozyme activated by GlcN-6-P — different from E. coli sRNA cascade
MreB paralogues: MreBH, Mbl in B. subtilis — partially redundant cytoskeletal proteins
Divisome 2024: Single sPG-track; PBP2B motion synthesis-driven, asynchronous with FtsZ
UptA: Newly discovered UndP inner-leaflet flipper completing carrier recycling cycle
Clinical & Research Relevance

PG-Targeting Antibiotics

From penicillin (1928) to clovibactin (2023), PG remains the most productive antibiotic target class. Understanding the enzyme–drug interaction mechanistically explains both efficacy and resistance.

Fosfomycin
Phosphonic Acid · Clinical 1969
Targets MurA. Covalently modifies Cys115 via irreversible Michael addition — permanently blocking the first PG-committed step. Re-emerging for UTIs, MRSA combination therapy, and MDR organisms.
Resistance: murA C115D mutation, FosA/B/C GSTs, reduced uptake (glpT/uhpT).
D-Cycloserine
D-Ala Analogue · Second-line anti-TB
Double block: inhibits both Alr (alanine racemase) and DdlA/B (D-Ala–D-Ala ligase) — depleting D-Ala for two sequential steps. Second-line anti-TB; renewed interest in MDR-TB regimens with bedaquiline.
Tunicamycin
Nucleoside · Research tool
Blocks MraY — prevents Lipid I formation. Eukaryote-toxic (inhibits N-glycosylation). Specific natural product inhibitors (mureidomycins, capuramycins) in preclinical development.
Bacitracin
Cyclic Polypeptide · Topical 1945
Forms Zn²⁺-chelate with UndPP — blocking dephosphorylation by BacA/PgpB/YbjG. Depletes the carrier lipid pool. Used topically; nephrotoxic systemically.
Vancomycin
Glycopeptide · Last resort since 1958
Binds D-Ala–D-Ala terminus of Lipid II (Kd ~1 μM), sterically blocking TGase and TPase. VRE resistance: VanA replaces D-Ala–D-Ala with D-Ala–D-Lac (~1000-fold reduced affinity).
VanA/B/C/D/E/G clusters → D-Ala–D-Lac or D-Ala–D-Ser. Oritavancin overcomes some VRE resistance.
Penicillins / Cephalosporins
β-Lactams · Since 1928
Mimic D-Ala–D-Ala transition state; covalently acylate the DD-transpeptidase serine of PBPs — blocking cross-linking. Different β-lactams have distinct PBP selectivity profiles.
Resistance: β-lactamase hydrolysis, PBP alteration (MRSA mecA/mecC), LD-bypass (LdtD overexpression).
Carbapenems
Broad-spectrum β-Lactams · 1985
Uniquely inhibit both PBP DD-transpeptidases AND LD-transpeptidases (LDTs) — closing the LD-bypass escape route. Last resort for MDR Gram-negatives. Carbapenemases (KPC, NDM, OXA-48) are the most alarming global resistance mechanism.
Mecillinam / Aztreonam
Selective β-Lactams
Mecillinam: PBP2-selective — cells become spherical (diagnostic of elongasome inhibition). Used for UTIs. Aztreonam: PBP3-selective monobactam — cells form filaments (cannot divide). Critical research tools.
Moenomycin
Phosphoglycolipid · TGase inhibitor
Binds the donor site of TGase in Class A PBPs — mimics the glycan chain product. Different mechanism from vancomycin. Poor pharmacokinetics limit clinical use but invaluable as a biochemical probe.
Teixobactin Nature 2015
Cyclic Depsipeptide · Novel class
Isolated from uncultured Eleftheria terrae via iChip. Binds both Lipid II and Lipid III (WTA precursor) via conserved MurNAc-PP moiety — simultaneously blocking PG and teichoic acid synthesis. Forms supramolecular fibrils disrupting membrane integrity. No resistance detected after 27 days serial passage in S. aureus or M. tuberculosis.
Active vs. Gram-positives and M. tuberculosis. Binding to lipid (not protein) makes resistance evolution nearly impossible.
Clovibactin Cell 2023
Depsipeptide · Novel class
Isolated from uncultured bacteria. Uses a hydrophobic cage to wrap around the pyrophosphate moiety of C55PP, Lipid II, and Lipid III-WTA — bypassing variable structural elements. Forms supramolecular fibrils (confirmed by solid-state NMR + AFM). No resistance detected. Active against MRSA and VRE in mouse models.
Pyrophosphate cannot be mutated without destroying function — making resistance evolution essentially impossible through target modification.
Ramoplanin
Lipoglycodepsipeptide
Binds Lipid II at a site distinct from vancomycin's D-Ala–D-Ala — so it retains activity against VRE. Also inhibits MurG. Forms fibrils on the membrane. Reached late-stage trials for C. difficile infection.
Colicin M
Bacteriocin · Enzymatic
Unlike every other agent here, colicin M is an enzyme: a phosphodiesterase that hydrolyses the pyrophosphate bond of Lipid I and Lipid II, destroying the precursor and irreversibly consuming the carrier lipid. Produced by E. coli strains to kill competitors.
Bulgecin A
Glycopeptide · LT inhibitor · Potentiator
Inhibits lytic transglycosylases (primarily soluble Slt70). Not antibacterial alone, but strongly potentiates β-lactams — it prevents the cell from clearing damaged PG, converting a bacteriostatic insult into lysis. A key research tool for probing LT function.
Illustrates the potentiator concept: block remodelling, and existing antibiotics work better.
Cefsulodin · A22/MP265 · CDFI/DMPI
Research tool compounds
Cefsulodin: selectively inhibits the TPase domain of aPBPs. A22 and MP265: depolymerise MreB, collapsing the elongasome and rounding cells. CDFI/DMPI: small-molecule MurJ inhibitors that block Lipid II flipping. Essential for dissecting which machine produces which phenotype.
Copper ions (Cu²⁺)
LDT inactivator
Cu²⁺ inactivates LD-transpeptidases at concentrations below the MIC — impairing envelope stability in E. coli and, importantly, preventing PBP bypass in E. faecium. A reminder that the LD route has chemical vulnerabilities beyond carbapenems.
Complestatin & Corbomycin
Glycopeptides · Novel MOA
Entirely new mechanism: bind directly to the PG sacculus itself, blocking hydrolase access. Cells cannot open the mesh for new PG insertion → growth arrest. First antibiotics that kill by blocking PG hydrolases rather than synthases.
Kill by preventing PG remodelling — not PG synthesis. Novel mechanism class highlighting PG hydrolases as drug targets.
Part IV

PG Modifications & Diversity

The canonical GlcNAc–MurNAc–pentapeptide is a starting point, not the finished product. Post-synthetic tailoring of both the glycan backbone and the stem peptide underlies lysozyme resistance, immune evasion, sporulation, and β-lactam resistance in several major pathogens.

Modification Systems — click for detail
Glycan: OatA · PatA/PatB · PgdA · NamH Peptide: MurT–GatD · AsnB · δ-lactam NCDAAs & FDAA probes Teichoic acids (WTA/LTA · Lipid III)
🧬
Position 3 Variation
mDAP: most Gram-negatives, Bacillus, mycobacteria. L-Lys: S. aureus, S. pneumoniae — requires an interpeptide bridge of 1–7 residues. L-Orn and L-diaminobutyrate occur in other taxa. This single position dictates the entire crosslinking strategy.
🌉
Interpeptide Bridges
Pentaglycine in S. aureus, built by FemX, FemA and FemB from glycyl-tRNA — not by ribosomes. L-Ala–L-Ala or L-Ser–L-Ala in S. pneumoniae. D-Asp in E. faecium. Fem enzymes are attractive MRSA targets since bridge loss abolishes methicillin resistance.
🛡️
Lysozyme Evasion
O-acetylation of MurNAc C6 (OatA, PatA/PatB) and de-N-acetylation of GlcNAc (PgdA) both sterically block lysozyme. Both also reduce NOD-receptor recognition — one modification, two benefits. E. coli and Salmonella largely lack these.
🦠
Mycobacterial PG
Contains mDAP with no interpeptide bridge, but carries a unique N-glycolyl-MurNAc modification (NamH) that shapes host immune responses. Crucially, mycobacteria are LD-crosslink dominant (3→3 predominates), and PG is covalently linked to arabinogalactan and mycolic acids.
🌱
Spore Cortex PG
In B. subtilis and C. difficile, CwlD and PdaA convert MurNAc into muramic δ-lactam — a spore-specific modification essential for germination, because germination-specific lytic enzymes recognise it. Cortex PG is also far less crosslinked than vegetative PG.
⚡
Amidation & MRSA
MurT–GatD amidates iso-D-Glu to iso-D-Gln — essential in S. aureus and required for efficient PBP2 crosslinking. Disrupting it re-sensitises MRSA to β-lactams, making the amidation machinery a genuine adjuvant target.
Analytical Toolbox

Studying PG: Muropeptide Analysis

Nearly everything on this page was established by a handful of methods. Understanding what each technique can and cannot resolve is essential to reading the primary literature critically.

Methods — click for detail
Peptidoglycomics (LC-MS/MS) FDAA labelling (HADA/NADA/TDL)

Three Levels of Compositional Analysis

1 — Directed subset
Search chromatograms for predefined m/z values of known muropeptides. Hypothesis-driven and fast. Successfully used to identify the LDT responsible for 1→3 crosslinks in Alpha- and Betaproteobacteria. Blind to anything you did not think to look for.
2 — Undirected subset
Take the most abundant peaks without prior assumptions — the classical HPLC approach. Glauner identified ~80 muropeptides in E. coli this way; Lee identified 20 in P. aeruginosa. Establishes the core composition but misses low-abundance species.
3 — Global peptidoglycomics
Feature-extract every m/z peak, annotate all of them, confirm by MS/MS. Several hundred muropeptides identified in P. aeruginosa versus 20 by classical subset analysis of the same organism. Detects modifications present at only 300–400 copies per cell.

Interpretation Caveats

Isobaricity: MS1 mass alone cannot distinguish 4→3 (DD) from 3→3 (LD) dimers — they are isobaric. MS/MS fragmentation or chemical derivatisation is required to assign crosslink type. This is the single most common source of over-interpretation in muropeptide papers.
Anhydro-muropeptides are a dual readout: changes in anhydro levels reflect BOTH lytic transglycosylase activity AND average glycan chain length. Attributing a shift to one alone is unsafe without independent chain-length data.
Core vs adaptive muropeptides: abundant 'core' species stay relatively constant; the biologically interesting low-abundance 'adaptive' species change with stress, host environment, and antibiotics — and are exactly what subset methods miss.
Sacculi vs released fragments: standard SDS-boiling isolates the sacculus. A validated method for purifying soluble released muropeptides compatible with peptidoglycomics does not yet exist — so the fragments actually reaching the host are still poorly catalogued.
Leaky enzymes: MurE can add amino acids other than mDAP, and MurC can accept Gly or Ser in place of L-Ala, at low efficiency. Unexpected peaks may reflect promiscuity rather than a dedicated pathway.
Pharmacological Dissection
Selective inhibitors let you isolate individual machines in vivo. Mecillinam (PBP2) → spherical cells, elongasome off. Aztreonam / cephalexin (PBP3) → filaments, divisome off. Moenomycin (aPBP TGase) → aPBP contribution removed while SEDS synthesis continues, since RodA and FtsW lack the moenomycin-binding donor cavity. Cefsulodin → aPBP TPase specifically. A22 / MP265 → MreB depolymerisation. Bulgecin → lytic transglycosylases. Combining two of these — for example a bPBP inhibitor with moenomycin — is the standard way to assign a phenotype to one synthase system rather than another.
Cutting Edge Science

Recent Research Highlights

PG biology has seen remarkable discoveries from 2017–2025 that reshaped our understanding of enzyme function, complex dynamics, and antibiotic development.

2017
SEDS Proteins (RodA, FtsW) Identified as Transglycosylases
Meeske et al. (2017, Nature) proved SEDS proteins are bona fide glycosyltransferases. Before this, only Class A PBPs were known PG polymerases. This revealed two independent PG synthesis systems — SEDS/bPBP-type and Class A PBP-type — with distinct regulation and sensitivity to inhibitors.
2021
Two-Track Model for Septal PG Synthesis (Yang et al., Nature Microbiology)
Single-molecule tracking of FtsW in E. coli: fast Z-track (~30 nm/s, inactive, follows FtsZ treadmilling) and slow sPG-track (~8 nm/s, active synthesis). FtsN converts Z-track to sPG-track. This explains how spatial distribution and synthesis activation are decoupled in the divisome.
2023
Clovibactin — Pyrophosphate Cage Mechanism (Cell 2023)
Clovibactin from uncultured bacteria targets the chemically immutable pyrophosphate moiety of C55PP, Lipid II, and Lipid III-WTA using solid-state NMR and AFM. Supramolecular fibril formation on bacterial membranes directly visualised. No resistant mutants detected. Active against MRSA and VRE in mouse models.
2024
Single sPG-Track in B. subtilis (Nature Microbiology 2024)
Live-cell single-molecule imaging of PBP2B in B. subtilis: only a single processive population driven by PG synthesis — not FtsZ treadmilling. The divisome complex is multimeric. FtsZ treadmilling partially influences PBP2B speed but motions are asynchronous. Reveals fundamental mechanistic differences between diderm (E. coli) and monoderm bacteria.
2024
UptA Identified as UndP Inner-Leaflet Flipper in B. subtilis
UptA identified as a dedicated flipper returning undecaprenyl phosphate (UndP) to the inner membrane leaflet after phosphatases act on UndPP. Completes the carrier recycling cycle — closing a long-standing gap in understanding how the carrier lipid resets for another round of synthesis.
2025
PG Recycling Re-mapped (Trends in Microbiology 2025)
A comprehensive review showed recycling is far more diverse than the AmpG-only model: alternative importers (YejBEF–YepA, MppA–OppBCDF), alternative LD-carboxypeptidases (LdcV, ElsL), and the MupP–AmgK–MurU bypass that regenerates UDP-MurNAc downstream of MurA/MurB — conferring intrinsic fosfomycin resistance. Mpl-mediated direct peptide reuse turns out to be the exception rather than the rule across bacteria.
2025
Global Peptidoglycomics (J. Bacteriol. 2025)
Anderson et al. formalised the distinction between directed subset, undirected subset and global compositional analysis. Global LC-MS/MS identified several hundred muropeptides in P. aeruginosa versus 20 by classical methods, and can detect modifications present at only 300–400 copies per cell — revealing low-abundance 'adaptive' muropeptides, such as the alaninol modification in S. Typhimurium persisting inside fibroblasts, that earlier workflows missed entirely.
2025
SagA Inhibitors Sensitise VREfm to Vancomycin — PG Hydrolases as Drug Targets
First-in-class covalent sulfonyl fluoride inhibitors of SagA (NlpC/P60 endopeptidase) impaired VREfm PG remodelling, reduced vancomycin MIC even with intact vanA, and improved outcomes in mouse sepsis models. Establishes PG hydrolase inhibition as a validated therapeutic approach to overcome antibiotic resistance.
2025–2026
PG Hydrolase Inhibitors — Landmark Review (ACS Infectious Diseases)
Synthesised progress on small-molecule inhibitors of PG hydrolases as a new antibiotic strategy. Killing by preventing remodelling (not synthesis) is orthogonal to all existing antibiotics — making hydrolase inhibitors candidates for combination therapies against MRSA, VRE, and MDR Gram-negatives. This represents the newest frontier of PG-targeted drug discovery.
